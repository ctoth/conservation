# Papers

## [Towards a theory of biological robustness](Kitano_2007_TheoryBiologicalRobustness/notes.md)  (robustness, trade-offs, bode-integral, systems-biology, conservation)
Kitano's 2007 editorial defines biological robustness as a probability-weighted integral of relative function preservation over a perturbation space and distinguishes it from stability and homeostasis. It poses, without proof, that over the entire (or a sufficiently broad) perturbation space with equiprobable weighting, the robustness difference between two tunings of the same system is zero, by analogy with the Bode sensitivity integral, MCA summation theorems, HOT, and the gain-bandwidth product, and leaves open whether such conservation exists "in either relative or strict manner". It is the primary source for the "conservation of robustness" framing whose conditions this project is checking.

## [Violations of robustness trade-offs](Kitano_2010_ViolationsRobustnessTrade-offs/notes.md)  (robustness, trade-offs, bode-integral, pareto-frontier, systems-biology)
Kitano argues, by analogy with Markowitz portfolio selection, that robustness trade-offs (robustness vs. fragility, robustness vs. performance) bind only on the efficient (Pareto) frontier, so a system not yet sufficiently optimized can become more robust without losing anything, illustrated by a series-vs-parallel resistor feedback amplifier (Box 1) and a hypothetical cell-culture mapping into yield-risk space. The paper has no theorem or data; it limits Bode's integral to frequency-axis sensitivity of negative feedback, adds in its supplement a separate feedback-gain vs. component-failure trade-off with "sloppy" components evolving under feedback compensation, and proposes artificial-evolution experiments to tell frontier-only from universal trade-offs. For the Bode coverage study it states the position that conservation holds only at optimality, with suboptimal designs strictly worse than the conserved line.

## [Multiplayer Performative Prediction: Learning in Decision-Dependent Games](Narang_2022_MultiplayerPerformativePredictionLearning/notes.md)  (performative-prediction, game-theory, distribution-shift, multi-agent-optimization)
Defines multiplayer performative prediction, an n-player game in which each player's data distribution D_i(x) depends on the joint action of all players, and studies performatively stable equilibria and Nash equilibria. Stable equilibria exist, are unique, and are found linearly by retraining or gradient methods when the aggregate sensitivity rho = sqrt(sum_i (beta_i gamma_i/alpha)^2) < 1; strong monotonicity (and hence Nash-seeking SGD, derivative-free and adaptive methods) holds when rho < 1/2 and cross-player effects are small relative to own effects. Relevant to whether an optimization budget is shared across several optimizers: the stability condition sums squared per-distribution sensitivities across players, and the monotonicity condition needs competitor effects to shrink like 1/sqrt(n-1), though everything is stated in Wasserstein-1/Lipschitz terms, not information.

## [Fundamental Limits for Sensor-Based Control via the Gibbs Variational Principle](Pacelli_2026_FundamentalLimitsSensor-BasedControl/notes.md)  (fundamental-limits, information-theory, stochastic-control, partial-observation, free-energy)
Derives a lower bound on the optimal cost of any causal output-feedback controller by applying the Gibbs variational principle to the closed-loop state/observation path measure against a reference measure in which observations are decoupled from state, giving J_irr(Ī) = sup_β{inf_u F^x_β(u) + J^u_trj(u) − Ī/β} ≤ J* for any MI budget Ī. Its key move is a cost-information certificate Ī(J) (a policy with cost ≤ J has bounded state second moments and so bounded sensor MI) closed into a fixed point J_sc = J_irr(Ī(J_sc)) that is unique, found by bisection, and strictly tighter than the TRIP-style bound that evaluates MI under the open-loop distribution; on a scalar LQG it captures over 80% of J* at σ_v ≈ 10, and on Dubins tracking it stays informative where the open-loop-MI bound is vacuous. Relevant as a formal instance of a performance limit whose channel information is set by the closed-loop distribution (control concentrates the state, which starves the fixed sensor of information), though the sensor map itself is fixed and not policy-dependent.

## [Performative Prediction](Perdomo_2020_PerformativePrediction/notes.md)  (performative-prediction, distribution-shift, strategic-classification, goodhart, fixed-point)
Formalizes predictions that change the distribution they are evaluated on as a map θ -> D(θ), assumes the map is ε-Lipschitz into Wasserstein-1 distance, and defines performative optimality (minimize risk on the induced distribution) versus performative stability (fixed point of retraining). Proves repeated risk minimization contracts with factor εβ/γ and converges linearly to a unique stable point iff-tightly when ε < γ/β, that stable points lie within 2L_z ε/γ of performative optima, and that performative risk can be concave even under all these assumptions. Gives the basic quantitative handle for Goodhart-style feedback where optimizing a model shifts its own evaluation data, though it only controls Lipschitz evaluation functions and uses the same loss for training and evaluation.

## [How much does your data exploration overfit? Controlling bias via information usage](Russo_2015_MuchDoesDataExploration/notes.md)  (adaptive-data-analysis, mutual-information, selection-bias, kl-divergence, sub-gaussian)
Russo and Zou prove that the bias of any data-dependent selection T among statistics φ_i is bounded by σ·sqrt(2·I(T;φ)) when each φ_i − μ_i is σ-sub-Gaussian, using the Donsker–Varadhan variational form of KL divergence applied per selected index, with extensions to unequal variances, absolute/squared error, and sub-exponential tails (where the bound becomes linear in I). They show matching lower bounds for argmax of Gaussians and threshold selection (including bias ≥ H(T)/2 for shifted exponentials), and use the framework to analyze filtering, rank selection, LARS, FDR, and randomized (Gibbs / Gaussian-noise) exploration. For this project it is the primary source for the "I nats of information buy at most σ·sqrt(2I) of overestimation" law and for evidence that exponential-or-heavier tails break the square-root scaling.
