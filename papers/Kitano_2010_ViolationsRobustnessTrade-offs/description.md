Kitano argues, by analogy with Markowitz portfolio selection, that robustness trade-offs (robustness vs. fragility, robustness vs. performance) bind only on the efficient (Pareto) frontier, so a system not yet sufficiently optimized can become more robust without losing anything, illustrated by a series-vs-parallel resistor feedback amplifier (Box 1) and a hypothetical cell-culture mapping into yield-risk space. The paper has no theorem or data; it limits Bode's integral to frequency-axis sensitivity of negative feedback, adds in its supplement a separate feedback-gain vs. component-failure trade-off with "sloppy" components evolving under feedback compensation, and proposes artificial-evolution experiments to tell frontier-only from universal trade-offs. For the Bode coverage study it states the position that conservation holds only at optimality, with suboptimal designs strictly worse than the conserved line.
