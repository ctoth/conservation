Defines multiplayer performative prediction, an n-player game in which each player's data distribution D_i(x) depends on the joint action of all players, and studies performatively stable equilibria and Nash equilibria. Stable equilibria exist, are unique, and are found linearly by retraining or gradient methods when the aggregate sensitivity rho = sqrt(sum_i (beta_i gamma_i/alpha)^2) < 1; strong monotonicity (and hence Nash-seeking SGD, derivative-free and adaptive methods) holds when rho < 1/2 and cross-player effects are small relative to own effects. Relevant to whether an optimization budget is shared across several optimizers: the stability condition sums squared per-distribution sensitivities across players, and the monotonicity condition needs competitor effects to shrink like 1/sqrt(n-1), though everything is stated in Wasserstein-1/Lipschitz terms, not information.
